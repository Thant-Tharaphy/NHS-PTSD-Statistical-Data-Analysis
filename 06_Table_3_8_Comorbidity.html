Table 3.8 — Health Conditions, Trauma and PTSD¶

Table 3.8 examines age-standardised estimates of trauma experience and PTSD screening positivity according to limiting physical health condition and common mental health condition (CMHC), with results shown by gender.

Key Findings¶

PTSD screening¶

For all adults, PTSD screening positivity was higher among those in the "Yes" group for both health conditions.

  • Limiting physical health condition

    • Yes: 10.09%
    • No: 3.44%
    • Difference: 6.65 percentage points

  • Common mental health condition

    • Yes: 22.23%
    • No: 1.07%
    • Difference: 21.16 percentage points

The largest PTSD difference was therefore observed between adults with and without a common mental health condition.

Trauma experience¶

Trauma experience was also higher among adults in the "Yes" groups.

  • Limiting physical health condition

    • Yes: 44.72%
    • No: 29.49%
    • Difference: 15.23 percentage points

  • Common mental health condition

    • Yes: 53.22%
    • No: 30.15%
    • Difference: 23.07 percentage points

Trauma vs PTSD Gaps¶

Condition Trauma Gap (pp) PTSD Gap (pp)
Limiting physical health condition 15.23 6.65
Common mental health condition 23.07 21.16

Gender Pattern¶

For trauma experience, women's estimates were generally somewhat higher than men's. However, among adults with a common mental health condition, the estimates were very similar:

  • Men: 52.60%
  • Women: 52.85%
  • Difference: approximately 0.25 percentage points

For PTSD screening, men had higher estimates than women within the "Yes" groups for both health-condition types.

Interpretation¶

Overall, both trauma experience and PTSD screening positivity were higher among adults with limiting physical health conditions and common mental health conditions than among adults without those conditions.

The differences were particularly large for common mental health condition status.

These results are descriptive age-standardised estimates and show associations between health-condition status, trauma experience and PTSD screening. They should not be interpreted as evidence of causation or statistical significance.
